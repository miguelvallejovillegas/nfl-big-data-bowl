# Speed That Translates: Rushers Fast on the Stopwatch and on Sundays Beat Their Draft Slot

**We laid every 2023–25 defensive lineman's tracked Combine 40 start over his own random NFL pass-rush starts. Once size is removed, the shape of the start does not carry over. Agreement does. Rushers fast on the official 40 *and* in about 20 NFL starts beat a pressure forecast from draft slot, weight and role 8 times in 10, against 16 in 59 for everyone else, on classes the forecast never saw.**

![Figure 1. Six rushers: Combine 40 start (orange) over his own NFL starts (blue; thin lines are single snaps), first step shaded. Right: career pressure rate minus the held-out forecast.](figures/fig1_six_rushers.png)

## So what for a team

1. **Pro personnel: keep a "speed that translates" shortlist.** After about 20 random rushes, flag rushers with a top-third NFL first step and a top-half 40 time. On held-out classes they produced +5.0 pressures a season over forecast; everyone else, −1.4. Use it to hand out third-down snaps and to order extension talks.
2. **Draft room: pay for the stopwatch, not the trace.** The official 40 adds pressure beyond draft slot; the tracked 40 start adds nothing.
3. **Tape: a fast start alone is not a buy.** A top NFL first step without the 40 time is a hands-and-counter question (Maason Smith, below).

## The metric

**First step = how fast a lineman is moving 0.3 to 0.5 s after he first moves.** In that window the 40 and a pass rush are the same movement: a big man leaving a stance. By 1.5 s they part (median 6.8 yd/s in the 40, 3.1 on a rush, because he has met a blocker). Both are in standard deviations after weight and role (share of snaps at EDGE). **Translation** asks whether the Combine number previews the NFL number.

**Data.** Every tracked Combine 40 rep, standardized within year. NFL: random sampled pass plays only (the top-EPA half oversamples sacks), alignment-adjusted: 1563 starts by 85 linemen, median 19 each, reliability 0.64, tracking NGS get-off snap by snap (ρ = 0.51). Outcome: career pressures per rush for 69 rushers with 100+ rushes. **Baseline without tracking:** draft pick, weight and role; the stronger one adds the official 40 time and season NGS get-off.

## Finding 1: the Combine start does not translate

Raw, the 40 ranks NFL speed at 1.5 s at 0.57, but that is weight (heavier linemen are slower on Sundays, ρ = 0.82, and in the 40, 0.65). After weight and role, the 40 first step previews the NFL first step at ρ = 0.07 [−0.14, 0.28] (n = 67). No tenth of a second exceeds 0.11 (Figure 2A); corrected for measurement noise the link is at most 0.47. The pass-rush drill start does no better (0.04, n = 84). Five per-player similarity scores (gap, two speed ratios, curve shape, distance) never exceed 0.18 in rank correlation with pressure beyond pick, and carry-over fails on held-out classes (Table 1).

## Finding 2: two signals that agree beat the forecast

What carries is the official 40 time, +0.9 points per sd [0.4, 1.5] in 3 of 3 classes, and the NFL first step, +0.8 [0.1, 1.4]. They overlap: with the 40 and NGS get-off in the forecast, the first step keeps +0.4 [0.0, 0.9]. So we ask one football question: does the speed he showed in Indianapolis show up when he leaves his stance on Sundays?

**The rule.** *Both* = NFL first step in the top third and 40 time in the top half, with each class's cut-offs set on the other two classes.

**The test.** A rusher "hits" if his career pressure rate beats the held-out forecast; the base rate is 35%. Both hits 80% (8 of 10), all others 27% (16 of 59): a gap of 53 points [27, 75], shuffle p = 0.009. Dropping any one shortlisted rusher leaves at least 78%. By class: 3 of 4 (2023), 5 of 5 (2024), 0 of 1 (2025).

![Figure 3. A: every rusher with a 40, placed against his own class's held-out cut-offs; top right is the shortlist. B: shortlist hit rate against everyone else and the base rate, under five checks.](figures/fig3_shortlist.png)

**Checks (Figure 3B).** Shrunken forecast: 8 of 10 against 14 of 59. The first step is read from sampled snaps, so we removed all 40 per rusher from the outcome: 7 of 10 against 16 of 59, so it is not scoring its own pressures. The edge shrinks when the forecast already knows the 40 time and NGS get-off (6 of 10 against 15 of 59, p = 0.08) and on the rarer quick pressures and sacks (6 and 5 of 10; p = 0.25, 0.26). The lift is the combination, not a new athlete score.

## Why it works: the same rusher, faster snap

On 1279 random rushes by 69 linemen (120 pressures), a first step one sd faster than *his own* average multiplies the odds of pressure on that snap by 1.49 [1.25, 1.77]. His fastest fifth of starts ended in pressure 14.8% of the time, his slowest fifth 6.6% (Figure 4). It holds with that snap's NGS get-off, sideways movement, down and alignment controlled (1.51), and for EDGE (1.55) and interior (1.41). Reach the blocker's set point first and the tackle opens early. The 40 says the speed exists; the NFL starts say he uses it from a stance.

![Figure 4. Same rusher, faster snap: pressure by first-step fifth, and odds ratios across controls, classes and roles.](figures/fig4_snap_mechanism.png)

**Price.** Clubs already pay for the 40: one sd faster goes 50% earlier in pick number after weight and role (n = 89), while a fast tracked start goes 17% *later* if anything (p = 0.07). Since the 40 still adds +0.9 beyond the pick, clubs appear to under-pay for it (an association).

**Where the rule disagrees with the tape: Maason Smith.** Jacksonville's 2024 second-rounder has the cohort's best NFL first step, a 48th-percentile 40, and 3.9% pressure against a 7.7% forecast; only 18% of his pressures came quickly. His curve is fast through half a second, then flat (Figure 1): he arrives, he does not win. His starts are among the straightest (25th percentile sideways), so stunts do not explain it. The coaching fix is hands and a counter, not more get-off work. **The shortlist miss: Jordan Burch** (2025, Arizona) sits top right at 5.8% against 8.5% after one season; as the only 2025 rusher on the list, the cleanest class has not tested the rule yet.

## Limitations

- **Not blind.** The two-signal form was chosen in an earlier round after seeing all three classes; only the cut-offs are held out. The rule is frozen and hashed (`3726d741954d`, `frozen_rule.json`); 2026 is its first true test.
- **Ten rushers.** The shortlist is small, its 90% interval wide (Figure 3B), and nine of ten come from 2023–24. One new class gives the slope test only 47% power.
- **Translation itself is a null.** The tracking contribution is the NFL first step; the Combine contribution is the stopwatch. DL was the clearest of five groups screened; for WR, OL, DB and TE the NFL start cannot be read reliably from about 20 sampled snaps.
- **Sampled tape, no blockers.** A slow start can be a two-gap call, a fast one a free rush. A forward test (rookie-year first step against later pressure) has 23 rushers and is inconclusive (p = 0.52).
- **Outcome.** Pressure is charted and usage-dependent; injuries are not modelled.

## Appendix

**Slope test (Table 1).** The forecast, fit on two classes, is a fixed logit offset in a quasi-binomial model of the held-out class's pressures out of rushes; class slopes are pooled with random-effects shrinkage; p shuffles the measure within class 2,000 times. The first-step window was read off the payoff curve in an earlier round; the best of 11 windows under the same shuffle gives p = 0.02. Rerunning everything on resampled rushers gives [0.2, 1.2]; dropping any one rusher, 0.6 to 0.9; a ridge forecast, +0.8. Ridge (penalty chosen inside training classes) does not improve calibration (held-out slopes 1.23, 1.89, 0.63 against 1.07, 1.42, 0.61; 1 is ideal), so least squares stays primary. **Shortlist.** The gap interval resamples rushers within class; the shuffle p permutes the Both label within class 2,000 times. **Sample.** 40 first-step repeatability 0.54 (n = 56); sampled-snap pressure tracks career pressure at ρ = 0.26.
